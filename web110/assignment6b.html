Accessibility

The first activity I did was review 'Mozilla: What is Accessibility?'. What stood out to me from this reading is the sheer amount of variety when it comes to the types of needs based on specific disabilities that people may have. They can be categorized into visual, auditory, motor, and cognitive. Another interesting takeaway for me was the fact that accessibility is more difficult and expensive the later you implement it to anything including a website. Accessibility features therefore are best to design in the early stages of building a website. The next activity I did was explore color schemes in Paletton website. From an accessibility point of view, I was looking for color schemes that had the most contrast with lightness and darkness. For color blindness, it is known that it is hard to discern between hues, so in order to simulate this I kept checking to see if the colors were hard to tell apart when turned to gray scale. I found that blue and yellow had the most contrast when turned to gray scale. Knowing which two colors contrast well with each other is a good starting point for creating color schemes for colorblindness.
